Russia, Ukraine and the US ended two days of talks in Abu Dhabi with a deal on the largest prisoner exchange since 2022 but no ceasefire; Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were due in Moscow next (BBC, AP, NPR).

US prosecutors charged three Iran-linked operatives over an alleged attack on RAF Fairford in Britain and a dozen attempted attacks across the US and Europe (CBS). Donald Trump said the US had "already won" its war with Iran and wanted "total victory"; Pete Hegseth said forces were "locked and loaded" (Times of Israel). Oil slipped to a two-month low.

El Salvador's helicopters fired rockets at a compound in Haiti it called the headquarters of the 400 Mawozo gang; Haiti's prime minister said "hats off to Bukele", and gang leader Wilson Joseph was not there (BBC).

An Israeli strike hit the intensive-care unit of Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, killing at least six and wounding 20, staff among them, Gaza's civil defence said, more than a year into a ceasefire that has not stopped Israeli strikes (NYT).

Nvidia's board met over a scandal surrounding chief executive Jensen Huang, including a relationship with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy, and hired a law firm to investigate (CNN). The $4.2tn chipmaker is 6% of the S&P 500, which slipped with Asian markets as US October inflation came in hot (NDTV Profit).

Democrats' midterm wins reshaped Washington: Scott Colom won Mississippi's Senate seat, the first Black person elected to statewide office there since Reconstruction (CBS), and a House move to impeach Hegseth failed (BBC).

COP31's leaders' summit opened in Antalya with Recep Tayyip Erdogan and UN chief Antonio Guterres (AFP).

Indonesia's vice-president, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, resigned and President Prabowo Subianto accepted (Reuters). Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases and 5,387 deaths (AP). Nine were hurt when a bus crashed in Seongnam, South Korea (Yonhap). Russia's ambassador to Washington, Alexander Darchiev, died aged 66 (Euronews).
